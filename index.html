A Sacred Grass from the Banks of the Ganga

Moonj grass (Saccharum munja) grows wild on the fertile banks of the Ganga and its tributaries. Harvested annually in November, this sacred grass has been used for centuries by rural artisans to create durable, beautiful handcrafted products.

THE PROCESS

How Moonj Craft is Made

OI

Harvesting

Moonj grass is hand-cut from the riverbanks during the right season, when the stalks are strong and mature.

02

Drying

The grass is spread out in the sun and dried naturally for several days until it reaches the right flexibility.

03

Sorting & Splitting

Artisans sort the grass by thickness and split the strands by hand to get consistent, workable fibres.

04

Soaking

Before weaving, the fibres are soaked in water to make them pliable and easy to shape.

05

Weaving & Shaping

Using traditional techniques passed down over generations, artisans coil, weave, and bind the fibres by hand.

06

Finishing

Products are dried, trimmed, and quality-checked. Some pieces receive natural dyes or decorative accents.

07

Ready for You

Each piece is packed with care and sent on its way to a new home.

Kiran Devi

She has been weaving Moonj since before she could read. Known for the extraordinary tightness and evenness of her coils, Kiran's baskets last for decades.

Uttar Pradesh

Rama learned Moonj craft from her mother-in-law. She specialises in vintage baskets and grain containers, and is the quiet anchor of her artisan group.
